## Darcy's Law

Darcy found (_empirically!_) that flow thropugh pipes is proportional to two things: the difference in head over a specific length and the soil properties of the porous medium:

$$
Q = K \frac{\Delta h}{\Delta L} A
$$

where:
- $Q$ = volumetric flow rate [L^3/T]
- $K$ = hydraulic conductivity [L/T]
- $\Delta h$ = change in head [L]
- $\Delta L$ = length of flow path [L]

We can also write this in terms of the specific discharge (or Darcy flux):

$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = K \frac{\Delta h}{\Delta  L}
$$

Finally, note that Darcy's Law considers "average" flow through a porous medium. Note that porosity of the soil can be used to relate the specific discharge, $v$ to the _average pore velocity:_

$$
\bar{v} = \frac{v}{n}
$$

where $n$ is the porosity of the soil (volume of voids divided by total volume).

When is average pore velocity interesting? Consider, for example, the velocity of a contaminant moving with the groundwater in the pore space.

## Hydraulic Conductivity and Permeability

Hyraulic conductivity is defined using upper-case letter $K$. Permeability (a different parameter!) is defined using lower-case letter $k$. Note that while we will use these consistently in class, the is absolutely _not_ done in the "real world." The only way to know which one is being referred to is to pay attention to the units. Don't remember which is which? Take a look at the equations summarized below and see if you can figure it out yourself (it's good exam practice!).

See the reference textbooks (or lecture slides) for information on typical parameter ranges for $K$ and $k$.

Darcy's Law is, notably, an empirical one. However, many have attempted to describe $K$ and $k$ using physics-based models. For a complete discussion, Section 8.3 of Mitchell et al. (2025) (added to {ref}`References List <references>`).

The equations introduced in class were:

$$
Q = CD^2\frac{\gamma_w}{\mu} S^3 \frac{e^2}{1+e} iA
$$

where
- $C$ = shape factor for pore space (dimensionless) 
- $D$ = a (unspecified) representative grain size [L]
- $\gamma_w$ = specific weight of water [M/L^2/T^2]
- $\mu$ = dynamic viscosity of water [M/L/T]
- $S$ = degree of saturation (dimensionless)
- $e$ = void ratio (dimensionless)
- $i$ = hydraulic gradient (dimensionless)
- $A$ = cross-sectional area of flow [L^2]

A similar equation was also introduced for $k$:

$$
k = CD^2 S^3 \frac{e^2}{1+e}
$$

The discussion in class will not be repeated here, except to leave as an exercise for the reader to confirm the following key observations:

1. Identify which parameters are influcenced by soil properties (i.e., pores, fluid, etc.)
2. Identify which parameters are influcenced by fluid properties _only_
3. Recognize the elegance of Darcy's Law in that it is a simple equation that separates material properties from boundary conditions and geometry of the system (i.e., for now, simply the "pipe" from Darcy's experiments; this will obviously be made more complex later)

Note that there are several key "components" to our (future) groundwater problems that have now been introduced:

1. Soil properties (including geometry and composition of the porous medium)
2. Fluid properties
3. Geometry of the system (i.e., physical boundaries)
4. Boundary conditions of the water in the system
